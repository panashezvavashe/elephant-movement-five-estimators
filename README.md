# Comparative Analysis of Five Elephant Movement and Home-Range Estimation Methods

## Project Overview

This repository contains analytical workflows, geospatial processing scripts, and modelling procedures for comparing five approaches to estimating the spatial movement patterns and home-range distributions of African savannah elephants (*Loxodonta africana*) in Mana Pools National Park, Zimbabwe.

The study uses GPS telemetry data from 10 collared elephants, comprising 66,131 filtered GPS locations, to investigate how different movement-modelling techniques influence the estimation and interpretation of elephant spatial ecology.

## Estimation Methods

The project implements five complementary spatial estimation approaches:

1. **Autocorrelated Kernel Density Estimation (AKDE):** Estimates home-range distributions while explicitly accounting for temporal autocorrelation in animal movement data.

2. **Dynamic Brownian Bridge Movement Modelling (dBBMM):** Reconstructs movement pathways and estimates spatial utilisation distributions along observed movement trajectories.

3. **Adaptive Local Convex Hull (a-LoCoH):** Estimates home-range boundaries and identifies areas of concentrated space use using local convex hulls.

4. **Time-Geographic Density (TGD):** Examines spatiotemporal patterns of movement and the distribution of animal space use through a time-geographic framework.

5. **Elliptical Time Density (ETD):** Provides an alternative time-sensitive representation of elephant movement and spatial utilisation patterns.

## Research Objectives

- Compare home-range estimates generated by five different movement-modelling approaches.
- Assess differences in estimated spatial extent, home-range size, shape and core-use areas.
- Evaluate spatial agreement and concordance between estimation methods.
- Investigate temporal and seasonal variations in elephant space use.
- Determine how methodological choices influence ecological interpretation and wildlife monitoring decisions.

## Research Significance

The project contributes to movement ecology by demonstrating how estimator selection can influence the interpretation of elephant spatial behaviour. Its findings can support evidence-based wildlife monitoring, habitat management, conservation planning, and the identification of important elephant movement areas in Mana Pools National Park.

## Study Area

Mana Pools National Park, Zambezi Valley, Zimbabwe.

## Data and Reproducibility

The repository is intended to provide transparent analytical workflows and reproducible procedures for comparing the five estimation approaches. Access to sensitive GPS telemetry records may be restricted to protect wildlife locations.
